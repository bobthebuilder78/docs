Every person on your team gets one of five roles. The role controls what they can do across your organization.

## The five roles

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Organization Admin" icon="shield-check">
    Full access. The only role that can manage the team, billing, organization settings, and study ownership. Every organization needs at least one.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Organization Manager" icon="pen-to-square">
    Runs studies and sites day to day. Can create and edit studies, launch/pause/end them, share a study with another organization, manage sites, and work leads. Cannot touch billing, organization settings, team management, or study ownership.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Team Member" icon="user">
    Works the recruitment pipeline. Can update a lead's status, add notes and follow-ups, book and reschedule visits, reassign a lead to a different site, add a lead manually, and submit corrections to volunteer details for verification. Cannot change any study, site, billing, or organization setting.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Billing Admin" icon="credit-card">
    Sees everything, and additionally handles money: invoices, payment methods, wallet deposits, payments. Cannot change a lead, a study, a site, or organization settings. This is a separate capability track, not a higher or lower rung on the operational ladder.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Card title="Read-Only" icon="eye">
  Views everything within their scope. Writes nothing.
</Card>

A clinic with a front-desk coordinator, a study manager, and an office manager who handles invoices might set the coordinator up as a Team Member, the study manager as an Organization Manager, and the office manager as a Billing Admin — keeping billing separate from day-to-day study work.

## Inviting a team member

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Team settings">
    Go to **Settings → Team**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter their email and pick a role">
    There's no default role — you must choose one before the invite can be sent.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wait for acceptance">
    The invite is valid for 14 days. If it isn't accepted in that window, it expires and can be re-sent.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm they're on the team">
    Once accepted, the person shows up in your team list with the role you assigned.
  </Step>
</Steps>

You can change a person's role, or remove them from the team, at any time from the same page.

## Narrowing access with scope

Independent of role, a team member's access can be set to **all** studies and sites, or **restricted** to a specific set. A restricted person sees the union of whatever individual studies and sites they've been granted — nothing outside that, regardless of what their role would otherwise allow them to reach.

Scope only narrows _what a person can see_. It never grants a capability their role doesn't already have — a restricted Team Member still can't edit a study, for instance, even for a study they've been granted access to.

<Note>
  An Organization Admin can't be scope-restricted — their role already implies full access, and restricting it would create confusing gaps. The other four roles can all be scoped.
</Note>

## When your organization collaborates on someone else's study

If another organization shares a study with you, your team members' access to that specific study is capped by two things at once: their own role at your organization, and the collaboration tier your organization was given on that study. Whichever is more restrictive wins — neither substitutes for the other.

There are four collaboration tiers an organization can hold on a shared study:

- **Owner** — the organization the study belongs to.
- **Manager tier** — your Organization Admins and Managers can edit study setup and work leads. Your Team Members can still only work leads.
- **Recruiter tier** — nobody at your organization can edit study setup, even your Organization Admin. Everyone is limited to working leads (or viewing, depending on their own role).
- **Viewer tier** — everyone at your organization can view the study, but nobody can make changes.

<Note>
  If your organization is invited at Recruiter tier, your Organization Admin cannot edit that study's eligibility criteria or sites. The tier caps the whole organization, regardless of individual role.
</Note>

Your own sites, your own organization settings, and your own billing are never affected by a collaboration tier on someone else's study — those are always governed by your team members' roles at your own organization, not by any tier.
