A study always has exactly one owning organization. Any other organization's access to it comes from being invited as a collaborator at a specific tier. This is how a sponsor works with a CRO, how a research organization brings in an individual site, or how several independently-run clinics each work leads on studies owned by a central organization — while every organization involved keeps its own separate team and login.

Ownership itself never splits. Collaborators work within the tier they're given, and only a full ownership transfer moves the study to a new organization.

## The four tiers

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Owner" icon="crown">
    The organization the study belongs to. Full control, plus the only tier that can remove a collaborator, withdraw a pending invite, or accept an ownership transfer. Billing always follows the owner.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Manager" icon="pen-to-square">
    Everything Recruiter can do, plus can edit study setup, assign their own sites to the study, and invite further collaborators. Close to full parity with the owner, short of ownership itself.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Recruiter" icon="users">
    Can work leads: update lead status, book and reschedule visits, reassign a lead's site, add notes. Cannot touch study setup, sites, or invite anyone else.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Viewer" icon="eye">
    Read-only. Can see the study, its leads, and its collaborators. Cannot change anything.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Note>
  A tier caps the whole collaborating organization, not just one person. If your organization is invited at Recruiter tier, nobody there can edit study setup — not even their own Organization Admin. See [Roles & Permissions](/organizations/roles-permissions) for how this combines with a person's own role at their organization; the lower of the two always applies.
</Note>

## Inviting a collaborator

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the study">
    Go to the study you want to share, from your studies list.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Share the study">
    Search for the organization you want to invite. They need an existing Jaspari Studies account.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick a tier">
    Choose Manager, Recruiter, or Viewer. (Owner can't be assigned this way — it only moves through an ownership transfer, below.)
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send it">
    The invitation is valid for 14 days. The invited organization's admin has to accept it before their team gets access. If it isn't accepted in time, it expires and can be re-sent.
  </Step>
</Steps>

To invite a collaborator, your own organization must own the study or already hold Manager tier on it — a Recruiter- or Viewer-tier collaborator can't invite further collaborators.

## Billing always stays with the owner

The owning organization is billed for the study. This never changes based on how many organizations collaborate on it or which tier they hold. A Viewer-tier sponsor watching enrollment progress, a Recruiter-tier clinic working leads day to day, a Manager-tier CRO editing eligibility criteria: none of it moves the bill off the owner's account. A collaborating organization's own billing settings show no invoices or charges for a study it doesn't own, regardless of tier.

The only way billing responsibility moves to a different organization is a full ownership transfer.

## Transferring ownership

Sometimes a study needs to change hands entirely, not just gain a collaborator — for example, a CRO that set up a study on a sponsor's behalf handing it fully to the sponsor once they're ready to run it directly.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Invite first">
    The target organization has to already be an active collaborator on the study. You can't transfer ownership to an organization that isn't already collaborating.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Propose the transfer">
    Only the current owner's admin can propose it, and only to that already-invited collaborator.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The new owner accepts">
    Only their own admin can accept, and their organization needs a saved payment method and a wallet balance that clears their organization's deposit minimum first.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ownership moves">
    Once accepted, billing and study ownership move together to the new organization. The old owner keeps access, demoted to Manager tier — unless it was a placeholder organization with no real team, in which case its access is removed entirely.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Either side can cancel a pending transfer before it's accepted.

## Removing a collaborator

Only the owning organization's admin can remove a collaborator — this is stricter than inviting, which a Manager-tier collaborator can also do. Removing an organization cancels its in-flight visits on that study and ends its team's access immediately. It does not affect that organization's own studies, team, or anything it runs independently.

## When several clinics collaborate on one owner's studies

A common shape: one organization owns and runs a set of studies, and several separately-run clinics each collaborate on those studies, usually at Manager or Recruiter tier. Each clinic keeps its own team, its own roles, and its own sites. A clinic isn't billed for studies it collaborates on — it works leads and, at Manager tier, helps manage study setup, while the owning organization keeps billing and final control throughout.

<Tip>
  Invite each clinic at the tier that matches what they need to do day to day — Recruiter for a clinic that only works leads, or Manager if they also need to attach their own sites to the study.
</Tip>
