Every study has one geographic scope, set at the study level, that defines where volunteers must be located to match.

## Scope types

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Site Radius" icon="navigation">
    A radius around one of your assigned sites. Fits a study where volunteers need to travel to a physical location.
  </Card>

  <Card title="ZIP Radius" icon="map-pin">
    A radius around a specific ZIP code, independent of any site — useful when your recruitment area isn't centered on a physical location.
  </Card>

  <Card title="ZIP List" icon="list">
    A specific, hand-picked list of ZIP codes.
  </Card>

  <Card title="States" icon="landmark">
    One or more entire states.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Nationwide" icon="globe">
    The entire United States — typically used for virtual studies or very broad recruitment.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## How geography affects price

Your recruitment area's population factors into your study's per-lead price: a smaller population inside your chosen scope means a larger geography surcharge, since a smaller pool is harder to recruit from. See [Pricing](/billing/how-pricing-works) for how this combines with your eligibility criteria to set the final price.

## Adjusting scope

You can change your geographic scope any time the study is in draft, active, or paused. Common reasons to adjust:

- Your current radius or ZIP list isn't yielding enough volunteers.
- A protocol amendment changes how far volunteers can reasonably travel.
- You're expanding from a single-state to a nationwide virtual arm.

Changing your recruitment area recalculates your study's price the next time the study is (re)launched or otherwise deliberately edited — it doesn't retroactively change what you've already been charged for leads sent before the change.
